> **In plain English:** Background data is internet traffic an app uses while you are not actively using that app on screen.

You can close a messaging app and still receive a new-message notification. That is possible because the app or the phone's notification service can communicate in the background.

## Common examples

Background data may be used to:

- receive messages and email;
- sync photos;
- refresh weather information;
- download podcast episodes;
- update cloud files;
- preload social-media content;
- keep account information synchronized.

## Is background data bad?

Not automatically.

For a messaging app, background network access can be essential. For an app you rarely use, the same behaviour may be unnecessary.

The useful question is not “How do I stop all background data?” but “Which apps deserve to use it?”

## Can restricting it save mobile data?

Yes, particularly if an app frequently downloads content while closed. But the trade-off can be delayed notifications, stale information or backups that wait until Wi-Fi becomes available.

Restrict apps individually and test the effect rather than switching everything off at once.
