> **Quick answer:** If mobile data is turned on but your Android phone still cannot connect to the internet, first turn airplane mode on for about 10 seconds and turn it off again. Then confirm that the correct SIM is selected for mobile data, check that you still have an active data bundle, and restart your phone.

Seeing network bars on your phone does not always mean your internet connection is working.

Your phone may be connected to the mobile network while mobile data itself is unavailable, incorrectly configured or temporarily stuck.

The good news is that you usually do not need to reset your entire phone.

Work through these fixes in order.

## 1. Turn mobile data off and back on

Start with the simplest fix.

Open the quick settings panel on your Android phone and turn **Mobile data** off.

Wait about 10 seconds.

Turn it back on.

Then open a simple website or refresh the app that was not connecting.

Sometimes the mobile-data connection simply needs to reconnect to the network.

## 2. Turn airplane mode on briefly

If switching mobile data off and on does not work:

1. Turn on **Airplane mode**.
2. Wait about 10 to 20 seconds.
3. Turn Airplane mode off.
4. Wait for your network signal to return.
5. Turn mobile data on again.

This forces the phone to reconnect to the mobile network.

It is one of the fastest things to try when your phone shows signal but refuses to browse.

## 3. Check that you actually have data

A phone can display 4G or 5G even when your data bundle is finished.

Check your network balance before changing complicated settings.

For MTN Nigeria, see:

[How to check your MTN data balance](/internet/check-mtn-data-balance/)

For Airtel Nigeria, see:

[How to check your Airtel data balance](/internet/check-airtel-data-balance/)

If your balance is zero or your bundle has expired, buying another bundle may be all you need to do.

## 4. Make sure the correct SIM is being used for mobile data

This is especially important on dual-SIM Android phones.

You may have data on one SIM while your phone is trying to browse with the other.

Open your phone's settings and look for a section such as:

- SIM cards
- SIM manager
- Mobile network
- Connections
- Network & internet

Then look for **Mobile data** or **Preferred SIM for mobile data**.

Make sure the SIM with your active data bundle is selected.

The exact wording depends on your phone manufacturer.

Samsung, Tecno, Infinix, Xiaomi and other Android brands organize these settings differently, but the idea is the same.

## 5. Restart your phone

A restart sounds obvious, but it clears temporary network problems surprisingly often.

Restart the phone normally.

When it comes back on:

1. Wait for the network signal to appear.
2. Turn mobile data on.
3. Test your browser.

Do this before changing APN settings or performing a network reset.

## 6. Check whether the problem affects every app

Try opening more than one service.

For example:

- open a website in Chrome;
- send a WhatsApp message;
- refresh another app.

If only one app is failing while everything else works, your mobile data connection may not be the problem.

The affected app may be:

- temporarily unavailable;
- restricted from using mobile data;
- stuck in the background;
- experiencing its own server problem.

If every app fails, continue troubleshooting the connection itself.

## 7. Check whether Android is blocking mobile data for the app

Android can restrict individual apps from using mobile data.

Open:

**Settings → Apps → choose the affected app**

Look for something similar to:

- Mobile data
- Data usage
- Mobile data & Wi-Fi
- Background data

Make sure the app is allowed to use mobile data.

If you intentionally disabled background data earlier, remember that some apps need background access for notifications, syncing and updates.

You can also read:

[How to stop apps from using background data](/apps/stop-apps-background-data/)

## 8. Turn off Data Saver temporarily

Android's Data Saver feature limits how some apps use mobile data.

If an app suddenly stops connecting properly, temporarily disable Data Saver and test again.

The setting is usually somewhere under:

**Settings → Network & internet → Data Saver**

or:

**Settings → Connections → Data usage**

The exact location varies by phone.

If everything begins working after Data Saver is disabled, check which apps are allowed unrestricted data access rather than leaving every restriction off permanently.

## 9. Check your signal strength

One bar of signal can sometimes be enough for calls but not enough for a stable internet connection.

Try moving:

- closer to a window;
- outdoors;
- upstairs;
- away from a heavily enclosed room.

If mobile data begins working in another location, the problem is more likely poor network coverage than a phone setting.

Temporary congestion can also make a network appear connected while pages load extremely slowly.

## 10. Change your preferred network type

Your phone may be struggling to maintain a particular network mode.

Open your SIM or mobile-network settings and find **Preferred network type**.

Depending on your phone, you may see options such as:

- 5G/4G/3G/2G automatically
- 4G/3G/2G automatically
- LTE
- 3G

If 5G is unstable in your location, temporarily choosing a mode that includes 4G may give you a more reliable connection.

Avoid forcing an older network permanently unless you have a specific reason.

Automatic mode is normally the better everyday setting.

## 11. Remove and reinsert the SIM

If the connection still does not work:

1. Turn the phone off.
2. Remove the SIM card.
3. Check that it is positioned correctly.
4. Reinsert it.
5. Turn the phone back on.

This can help if the phone is having trouble reading or registering the SIM properly.

Be careful with the SIM tray and do not force it.

## 12. Test the SIM in another phone

This is one of the best ways to find out where the problem is.

Put the SIM into another compatible phone and test mobile data.

### If the data works in the other phone

The problem is probably with your original phone or its settings.

### If the data still does not work

The problem may be with:

- the SIM;
- the mobile network;
- your account;
- your data subscription;
- temporary service availability.

This test can save you from changing settings unnecessarily.

## 13. Be careful before changing APN settings

APN settings tell your phone how to connect to your mobile network's data service.

Incorrect APN settings can stop mobile data from working.

However, you should not start by copying random APN values from an old article or social-media post.

Network configurations can change.

First try:

- toggling mobile data;
- airplane mode;
- checking your balance;
- selecting the correct SIM;
- restarting;
- testing another location;
- testing the SIM in another phone.

Only investigate the APN after the simpler causes have been ruled out.

If you need APN settings, use the current information provided by your mobile network.

## 14. Reset network settings as a last resort

Android phones normally include an option to reset network settings.

It may be called:

- Reset Wi-Fi, mobile & Bluetooth
- Reset network settings
- Reset mobile network settings

Use this only after trying the simpler fixes.

A network reset can remove saved Wi-Fi networks, Bluetooth connections and other network preferences depending on your phone.

It does not normally erase your photos, apps or personal files, but read the confirmation screen carefully before proceeding.

## Why does mobile data show 4G but not work?

The 4G or 5G symbol tells you which type of mobile network your phone is connected to.

It does not guarantee that usable internet data is currently passing through that connection.

You can therefore see 4G while:

- your bundle is exhausted;
- the network is experiencing a temporary issue;
- the wrong SIM is selected;
- your phone has a configuration problem;
- signal quality is poor;
- an individual app has data restrictions.

That is why troubleshooting the problem step by step is more useful than judging the connection by the network icon alone.

## What should you try first?

Use this order:

1. Turn mobile data off and on.
2. Toggle airplane mode.
3. Check your data balance.
4. Confirm the correct SIM is selected.
5. Restart the phone.
6. Test multiple apps.
7. Check Data Saver and app restrictions.
8. Try another location.
9. Test the SIM in another phone.
10. Check APN or reset network settings only if necessary.

Start with the fixes that are quick and reversible.

You should rarely need to factory-reset an Android phone just because mobile data is not working.
