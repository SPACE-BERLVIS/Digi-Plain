> **Quick answer:** Slow charging is usually caused by a weak charger, damaged cable, dirty charging port, excess heat, heavy phone use while charging, or a battery/port problem. Start by testing a known-good charger and cable before changing software settings.

A phone can say it is charging while receiving much less power than it normally should. The fastest way to solve the problem is to isolate the power source, cable, port and phone one at a time.

## 1. Try a different wall socket

Plug the charger directly into another wall socket. If you were using an extension box, laptop USB port, car USB socket or power bank, remove it from the test.

Some USB ports provide enough power to charge eventually but not enough for fast charging.

## 2. Test another cable

Charging cables wear out internally even when the outside still looks normal.

If you have another cable that you know charges another device properly, test it with the same charger. If charging returns to normal, the cable is the likely problem.

Do not continue using a cable with exposed wire, a loose connector or signs of overheating.

## 3. Test the charger

The charger matters as much as the cable. A small low-power adapter may charge a modern phone slowly, particularly while the screen is on.

Use the charger that came with the phone or a reputable compatible charger that supports the power standard your phone expects.

## 4. Check the charging port

Pocket lint can become tightly packed inside a charging port and prevent the connector from sitting fully in place.

Shine a light into the port. If you can see debris, switch the phone off before cleaning. Avoid metal objects and liquids. If you are not comfortable cleaning the port, let a repair technician handle it.

## 5. Let the phone cool down

Phones can reduce charging speed when they become hot. This protects the battery.

Remove a heavy case, move the phone away from direct sunlight and stop gaming or recording video while it charges. Allow it to cool naturally; do not put the phone in a refrigerator or freezer.

## 6. Stop heavy use while charging

If the phone is consuming power almost as quickly as the charger supplies it, the battery percentage will rise slowly.

For a clean test:

1. Connect the charger.
2. Lock the screen.
3. Leave the phone untouched for 20 to 30 minutes.
4. Check how much the battery increased.

If charging is much faster while idle, heavy background or foreground use is contributing to the problem.

## 7. Restart the phone

A restart can stop a misbehaving app or system process that is keeping the processor active.

Restart the device, connect it to power and test again before installing cleaning or battery-boosting apps.

## 8. Check for moisture or port warnings

Some phones stop or limit charging when moisture is detected around the connector. If your phone displays a moisture warning, disconnect the charger and follow the manufacturer's drying instructions.

Do not try to force charging while the phone is warning about moisture.

## 9. Consider battery or port damage

If multiple known-good chargers and cables are slow, the problem may be inside the phone.

Common signs that professional inspection is sensible include:

- the connector only works at a particular angle;
- the phone repeatedly connects and disconnects from power;
- the port becomes unusually hot;
- the battery is swollen;
- charging remains slow even while the phone is switched off.

A swollen battery needs prompt professional attention. Do not puncture, squeeze or continue charging it.

## The fastest troubleshooting order

Use this sequence so you do not waste time:

1. Different wall socket.
2. Different cable.
3. Different compatible charger.
4. Inspect the port.
5. Let the phone cool and charge while idle.
6. Restart.
7. Seek hardware inspection if the problem remains.

That order tests the cheap, common causes before assuming the phone needs repair.
