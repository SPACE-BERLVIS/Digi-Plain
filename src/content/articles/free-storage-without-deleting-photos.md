> **Quick answer:** Start with downloads, large videos, offline media, app caches and duplicate files. Do not use a random “cleaner” app or delete folders you do not recognize just because they look large.

Running out of storage can slow updates, stop downloads and prevent the camera from saving new photos. You can often recover several gigabytes without touching your important pictures.

## Check what is actually using the space

Open your phone's storage settings first. On Android, search Settings for **Storage**. On iPhone, open **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**.

Look for the largest categories rather than guessing.

## Clear the Downloads folder

The Downloads folder often contains old PDFs, installation files, videos and documents that were needed once.

Open your file manager, sort Downloads by size and remove files you no longer need. If a document matters, move it to cloud storage or another device first.

## Remove offline music and video

Streaming apps can quietly keep large offline libraries.

Check apps such as YouTube, Netflix, Spotify and podcast players for downloaded content. Removing an offline copy does not usually remove the content from your account; it simply means you would need internet access to stream or download it again.

## Clear app cache carefully

Apps often keep temporary files to load faster.

On Android, many phones let you open an app's storage settings and clear its **cache**. Avoid choosing **Clear data** or **Clear storage** unless you understand that it can sign you out or reset local app information.

On iPhone, the system manages cache differently. Some apps offer their own cache controls, and iOS may suggest offloading apps that are rarely used.

## Review chat-app media

Messaging apps can accumulate years of forwarded videos, voice notes and duplicate images.

Use the storage-management feature inside the messaging app when available. This is safer than deleting random folders because you can usually see which chats or files are taking the most space.

## Find unusually large videos

A few long videos can consume more storage than hundreds of photos.

Sort your gallery by file size if your phone supports it, or use the storage manager to locate large media. Back up anything important before deleting it locally.

## Empty trash only after checking it

Deleted photos and files may remain in a recycle bin or recently deleted folder for days or weeks.

That is useful protection against mistakes, but it also means the storage is not fully recovered until the bin is emptied. Review it first.

## Avoid deleting system folders

Do not remove folders simply because their names look unfamiliar. Android apps and the operating system may depend on them.

A safer rule is to clean content through the app that created it, the phone's storage manager, or folders such as Downloads that you clearly understand.

## Create a repeatable storage habit

Once a month:

1. Review Downloads.
2. Remove old offline videos and music.
3. Check messaging-app storage.
4. Back up important photos and videos.
5. Empty trash after verifying the backup.

That prevents the emergency “storage full” warning from becoming a recurring problem.
