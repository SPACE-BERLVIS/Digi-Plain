> **Quick answer:** Use your phone's mobile-data usage screen to identify the apps consuming the most data, then restrict background data for non-essential apps. Do not block background access for apps whose real-time notifications you rely on without testing the effect.

Background data is internet traffic an app uses while you are not actively looking at it. Some background use is useful—messages, email and backups need it—but other apps can consume data unnecessarily.

## Find the biggest data users first

On Android, search Settings for **Data usage**, **Mobile data usage** or **App data usage**. The exact path differs between phone brands.

Review the list for the current billing period. Pay attention to apps you rarely open but that show unusually high usage.

## Restrict one app at a time

Open the app's data-usage details. Many Android phones provide a switch for **Background data** or a similar restriction.

Turn it off for apps that do not need to update when closed. Test the app afterward.

## Be careful with messaging and email apps

Blocking background activity can delay notifications from WhatsApp, Telegram, Gmail and similar apps.

If immediate messages matter, keep background access available or use your phone's less aggressive data-saving mode instead.

## Turn on Data Saver

Android's Data Saver feature limits background mobile-data use for many apps while allowing you to exempt important ones.

Search Settings for **Data Saver**. After enabling it, review the unrestricted-app list so essential services still work the way you expect.

## Stop automatic downloads on mobile data

Messaging, music, video and cloud-storage apps may download large files automatically.

Inside each app, look for settings related to:

- media auto-download;
- video quality;
- backups;
- offline downloads;
- automatic updates.

Choose Wi-Fi-only where that matches your needs.

## Check the result after a few days

Do not change twenty settings and assume the problem is solved. Reopen your data-usage screen after a few days and compare which apps are now responsible for most of the consumption.

That makes the process measurable rather than guesswork.
